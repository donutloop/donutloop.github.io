# ADR 0031 — Seeded planetary surface model (`src/planet.js`)

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: Accepted
Related: ADR 0022 (seeded RNG / determinism), ADR 0029 (spatial broadphase),
ADR 0030 (streaming budget), ADR 0016 (minimap + district labels)

## Context

Until now "the world" was three hard-coded predicates in
`ChunkManager.loadChunk`: `dist < 6 → city`, `|cx| ≤ 5 || |cz| ≤ 5 → highway`,
otherwise wasteland. That gives one city at the origin, two ribbon highways, and
an infinite grey void — the opposite of an ever-generating planet with cities,
lakes and woods. Biome "names" existed only as a `BIOMES` constant in
`minimap.js`, and nothing in the engine could answer "what is this place?".

Adding a planet means answering three questions at once — terrain, ecology and
settlement — and every one of them has to stay (a) deterministic from one seed,
(b) cheap enough for a per-frame streaming hot path, and (c) assertable from
Node without a GPU.

## Decision

Add `src/planet.js`: a **pure-data planetary surface model** with no THREE and
no DOM dependency, queried in chunk space, that is the single source of truth
for "what is this place".

1. **Scalar fields, not prefabs.** Elevation (continents + hills + ridged
   mountain ranges), moisture, temperature and a river-carving field are pure
   functions of world `(x, z)` built on a seeded integer hash (`hash2`) +
   value-noise fBm/ridged fbm. The planet owns its own hash stream so it stays
   reproducible even if another system touches the global RNG.

2. **Contrast, not more noise.** Raw fBm clusters around its mean; without
   `moistureContrast` / `temperatureContrast` / `continentContrast` the whole
   planet collapses into mild beige. With them one seed yields desert,
   rainforest, tundra, peaks and ocean in the same drive.

3. **Lakes pull the surface down; they are not subtracted.** Subtracting a
   basin term from an already-positive elevation never breaks the waterline
   (measured: 0 lake cells). `mix(e, -0.16, lake)` instead *replaces* the height
   inside isolated basins, so real lakes with beaches appear inland. Enclosed
   water is classified by a land-ring test (`isLake`) so lakes and sea can share
   the waterline maths but dress differently.

4. **Whittaker classification.** `biomeAt()` maps temperature × moisture (+
   altitude, water, shore and wetland overrides) onto 16 biomes. Per-biome
   rendering/ecology data (`ground` tint, tree density, flora mix) lives in
   `BIOME_TABLE`, so adding a biome is a data edit, not a renderer patch.

5. **Cities are chunk-snapped.** Settlements sit on a jittered lattice in
   **chunk** coordinates, so every city centre lands exactly on a chunk centre
   and the L-shaped inter-city legs run along chunk rows/columns. That keeps the
   existing axis-aligned highway builders, traffic lanes, parking and pedestrian
   systems working unchanged — a diagonal road network would have cost a rewrite
   of the whole transport stack for no player-visible gain.

6. **Cities are personalities, not palettes.** `CITY_KINDS` (metropolis,
   downtown, old town, industrial, suburbia, neon, harbour, agro, outpost,
   winter) each carry height range, density, tree cover, style weights, landmark
   type and palette. `kind` comes from the local climate + coastline + seed, so
   a harbour town means harbour town *because that is where the water is*.

7. **Deterministic engineering.** The origin cell is always a Metropolis, so the
   historic origin chunk (and its construction crane) survives, and the whole
   model is memoised per cell/chunk for the streaming hot path.

## Consequences

* `check_world.mjs` can now assert the *shape of the planet* — biome variety,
  ocean/lake/river presence, water fraction, determinism across instances,
  seed sensitivity, city profiles and road alignment — with no browser.
* Chunk classification collapses to one call, `planet.classifyChunk(cx, cz)`,
  which returns `{type: city|road|nature, biome, city, water, trees, flora,
  ground, height}`. The renderer and the population systems read the same object.
* Cities, rivers and lakes are now *places you can drive to*, which means
  follow-up work must keep them consistent: terrain flattening under cities and
  roads, bridges over rivers, and minimap/HUD naming all hang off this model.
* `groundHeight()` grades city cores and road corridors flat, so traffic and
  pedestrians never fight relief; wild terrain keeps its shape.
